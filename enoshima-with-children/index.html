Enoshima with children: ¥250 up the Escar, and the stairs back down

For parents taking young children to Enoshima for the first time: where the stroller stops, what the paid escalator covers, child fares at every gate, and when to turn back.

With children, Enoshima is two trips. The first runs from the bridge to the garden at the top of the island, and a paid escalator, the Escar (江の島エスカー), carries you up: ¥500 for an adult, ¥250 for a child at elementary school, free below that. The second runs down the far side to the sea caves, and it is stairs the whole way. The Escar only goes up.

So the plan is short. Ride up, let them run in the garden, and decide at the top. With a child who still has to be carried, turn back there. With walkers, go down to the caves only on a day when the Bentenmaru (べんてん丸), the small boat that brings you back, is sailing.

I have two daughters and I have never taken them to Enoshima. I think I’d like to go once, and I asked for a piece I could use myself. This is that piece.

It is desk research: nobody visited for it. Prices and hours come from the operators’ own pages, read on 27 November 2026. Where a parent blog or a guidebook is the only source, the text says so.

How far a stroller goes

From Katase-Enoshima Station (片瀬江ノ島) on the Odakyu line, the operator of the Escar and the garden gives 20 minutes on foot to the garden entrance on its access page, by the route that takes the Escar.

The first stretch is the Benten bridge (江の島弁天橋), then Nakamise (仲見世通り), the shop street that climbs to the red torii. Chikyu no Arukikata, a Japanese guidebook series, puts the shop street at about 200 m. Two Japanese parent blogs read for this piece say a stroller manages both. One of them calls the shop street a stone-paved slope that is packed with walkers at midday on holidays.

The stroller stops at Escar section 1, to the left of the red torii. The operator’s FAQ says you cannot take an open stroller on the Escar. You fold it and carry it, or you leave it at section 1, where staff keep it until the Escar closes at 19:05.

The FAQ names no fee, and a parent blog from April 2026 says storage is free. The FAQ does say that a stroller not collected by closing time is put out in front of section 1 and is no longer in the operator’s care.

Past section 1 the island is stairs. One of the blogs describes pushing a stroller up a side slope and giving up at a staircase after about 300 m. Bring a carrier for any child who cannot climb.

The Escar: three sections, and no way down

The Escar is an outdoor escalator in 3 sections, and its last section ends at the garden entrance. It lifts you 46 m in about 4 minutes.

It runs from 8:50 to 19:05 and takes cash, credit cards, transport IC cards and PayPay.

The other way up is the stone stairs. Chikyu no Arukikata counts about 250 steps for the same climb, and a parent blog puts it at about 20 minutes.

These fares date from 1 October 2025. Two Japanese listing sites read for this piece still show the old ¥360 and ¥180.

There is no Escar down. The FAQ says everyone uses the stairs on the way back.

The top: free until 17:00, with a catch from 1 December

The Escar ends at Enoshima Samuel Cocking Garden (江の島サムエル・コッキング苑). Entry to the garden is free from 9:00 to 17:00, and free all day when no event is on.

Inside is the Sea Candle (江の島シーキャンドル), a lighthouse tower with a glass-walled floor 41.75 m above the ground. It costs ¥800 for an adult and ¥400 for an elementary-school child. Todai Kitchen, a cafe listed on the operator’s website, says it has baby chairs.

Straight ahead from the garden entrance, in front of the Sea Candle, is the Fuwafuwa Dome (ふわふわドーム), a bouncing dome for children aged 3 to 6. Its page lists 10:00 to 19:30 and says it can be shut depending on the weather. The page names no fee.

If you want the Sea Candle, buy the set ticket at Escar section 1. It covers the Escar, the garden and the Sea Candle for ¥1,100, or ¥550 for a child. Paying for the Escar and the Sea Candle one by one costs ¥1,300 and ¥650.

The catch is the evening. The winter illumination runs from 1 December 2026 to 28 February 2027, and on those evenings the garden charges ¥500 and ¥250 from 17:00. The ticket page says leaving after 17:00 is charged as well, and the set ticket covers you until 17:00. In December, be out of the garden gate before 17:00, or stay for the lights and pay.

Down to the caves, and the Bentenmaru back

Past the garden, everything is downhill on foot. A 2023 walking course by the travel publisher Rurubu gives 9 minutes from the garden to its next stop and 5 more to the rock shelf at Chigogafuchi (稚児ヶ淵). The caves are 3 minutes further. The course is not timed for children.

Kanagawa Prefecture’s tourism page describes the walk from the bridge to the caves as about 40 minutes of going up and down stairs.

One English family guide, dated March 2026, says that with the escalator “even children can easily reach the cave”. The Escar stops at the garden. The caves are the stairs after it.

The Enoshima Iwaya caves (江の島岩屋) cost ¥500 from junior-high age and ¥200 for elementary-school children. The tourist association’s page lists 9:00 to 17:00 and says the hours change with the season. The first cave goes 152 m into the rock and the second 56 m. A listing supervised by the city’s tourist centre says there are no toilets inside.

From the caves there are two ways back. One is up the stairs you came down, then down the other side to the bridge. The other is the Bentenmaru, which sails between Chigogafuchi and the Benten bridge in about 6 minutes.

It is cash only. The prefecture’s English page still shows an adult fare of ¥400.

The tourist association gives rough hours: about 11:00 to 14:30 on weekdays, and about 10:30 to 14:30 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays. Ticket sales stop 15 minutes before the end.

In December, January and February there are no weekday sailings, with three exceptions: weekdays are due to run from 1 to 13 December, from 24 to 30 December, and from New Year’s Day to Monday 11 January. The page prints no year. The weekdays it gives match this winter.

Whether the Bentenmaru sails at all is settled at about 10:30 each morning and posted on the association’s site. Strong wind, high waves or a low tide cancel it, sometimes partway through the day. Ask before you bring a stroller on board: a large one, or one that does not fold, is refused.

So check after 10:30, before you start down. If the Bentenmaru is sailing, walk down, see the caves, and be at the Chigogafuchi landing before about 14:15. If it is cancelled, a child who walked down has to walk back up.

One more thing. The Bentenmaru lands at the Benten bridge. A stroller left at Escar section 1 is still at the top of the shop street, and somebody has to go up and collect it before 19:05.

Feeding and changing

Do both before you cross the bridge.

Katase-Enoshima Station lists diaper-changing equipment and baby chairs on its station page, and Odakyu’s family site lists a Mamaro baby-care booth there as of March 2025.

Enoshima Aquarium (新江ノ島水族館), 3 minutes on foot from the station, has two nursing rooms with hot water for formula and a microwave, one Mamaro booth, and changing sheets in several of its toilets. They are inside the building, so count them as part of an aquarium visit.

For the island itself, the official pages say nothing. The operator’s facility pages and FAQ mention no nursing room and no changing table.

Two parent blogs, first written in 2019 and 2020, describe a nursing room in the toilets of the cafe building beside the garden entrance. One says it is inside the women’s toilet, and the other calls it a single room with a lock. The 2020 blog adds that most public toilets on the island have a changing table.

A third blog, from April 2026, repeats the nursing room as something other visitors reported and warns that the facilities may have changed. Plan as if there is none.

What a family pays

Take 2 adults and 2 children at elementary school, starting from Fujisawa Station (藤沢).

The Odakyu line to Katase-Enoshima took 6 minutes on the train checked for this piece. An adult pays ¥170 by ticket or ¥167 by IC card. A child pays ¥90 by ticket and ¥50 with a child’s IC card, because Odakyu charges children a flat ¥50 anywhere on its lines. Up to 2 children below school age ride free with each paying passenger.

The Enoden is the other train from Fujisawa. It costs ¥220 and ¥110 with no IC discount, and the operator’s access page puts its Enoshima Station (江ノ島) 5 minutes further from the garden.

With paper tickets the trains are ¥1,040, and to the top and back is ¥2,540.

The Enoshima 1day ticket is ¥1,550 and ¥700. It covers the Escar, the garden, the Sea Candle and the caves, and it is sold at Escar section 1, at the Katase-Enoshima tourist information office, and online. For this family it saves ¥800 against paying at each gate, and ¥200 against set tickets plus cave tickets.

The aquarium’s page lists an Enoshima 1day passport, bought at Odakyu stations, under its discounts for showing a ticket: ¥200 off for adults and high-school students, ¥100 off for younger children. Do not count on free entry.

Skip the Sea Candle, and the same day with the caves and the Bentenmaru is ¥5,168.

With two children below school age, the train, the Escar and the Sea Candle cost them nothing, and to the top and back is ¥1,668. Watch the birthdays. The Escar and the train are free until a child starts elementary school, and the Bentenmaru charges ¥200 from age 6. The association’s page gives no price for the caves below school age.

What is not good

The way down is stairs for everyone. No page read for this piece shows a lift, a down escalator or a stroller route off the top. One parent blog calls the stairs to the caves steep and says it cannot recommend them with a baby or a toddler.

No official page confirms a nursing room on the island.

The Bentenmaru sails for three and a half to four hours a day, has no sailings on most winter weekdays, and can be cancelled after you are already at the bottom.

From 1 December, the garden’s free daytime ends at a gate that charges you to leave after 17:00.

Black kites take food along this coast. Fujisawa City’s page, updated on 24 November 2026, says they swoop on people holding food near the shore. It tells you to eat under a roof or with your back to a wall. The prefecture’s page says they come from behind and that their claws can injure.

Do not hand a child a snack on the bridge.

Parking is thin. The operator’s access page says holidays are very crowded and asks visitors to come by public transport.

The tourist association’s car park on the island has 74 spaces at ¥400 an hour with no daily cap. Its page says not to queue when it is full.

The prefecture’s underground car park at Katase Kaigan (片瀬海岸地下駐車場) has 200 spaces, and its page recommends it for Enoshima. Outside July and August it costs ¥210 per 30 minutes, capped at ¥1,710 once you pass 4 hours, and it is open 9:00 to 18:00 on weekdays and 7:00 to 19:00 on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays. On those hours it shuts before the Escar does. Five hours costs ¥2,000 on the island and ¥1,710 underground.

Rurubu’s listing says the caves close or change their hours in typhoons and rough weather.

Some things were not checked for this piece. A bus from Fujisawa Station stops 8 minutes on foot from the garden entrance, by the operator’s page, and a parent blog says it runs onto the island. Its fare is not here. Neither are motorcycle parking, the number of steps between the top and the caves, or how any of these times stretch at a small child’s pace.

If it rains or blows

The weather that cancels the Bentenmaru can also shut the dome and the caves. The fallback is Enoshima Aquarium, 3 minutes from Katase-Enoshima Station.

It costs ¥2,800 for an adult, ¥1,800 for a high-school student, ¥1,300 for elementary and junior-high students, and ¥900 for children aged 3 and over. The family of four above pays ¥8,200, which is more than the whole island day. Strollers are lent at ¥300.

The aquarium is open 9:00 to 17:00 until 30 November and 10:00 to 17:00 from 1 to 25 December, with last entry at 16:00. It is closed from 18 to 24 January.

The plan in five lines

  1. Feed and change at the station.
  2. After 10:30, check whether the Bentenmaru is sailing.
  3. Leave the stroller at Escar section 1 and keep the carrier.
  4. Decide at the top: garden and home, or caves and the Bentenmaru by 14:15.
  5. Collect the stroller before 19:05.

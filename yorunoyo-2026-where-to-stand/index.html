Yorunoyo 2026: nine free light shows a night, and where to stand

For anyone planning a December evening on Yokohama's waterfront: the nine show times, the places to watch from, what only the 17:00 show has, and the train home.

Yorunoyo (ヨルノヨ) lights Yokohama’s central waterfront from 4 to 30 December 2026, 17:00 to 21:05 every night, and entry is free. The part to time is Highlight of Yokohama (ハイライト・オブ・ヨコハマ), a 5-minute show of light and music across 50 sites on the waterfront. It starts on the hour and the half hour, 9 times a night.

Stand in Yamashita Park (山下公園) at 17:00, the only show with a ship’s horn. Then walk to the rooftop of Osanbashi (大さん橋), the organiser’s main viewpoint, for the next one. The train from Yokohama Station is ¥460 return.

I have never seen Yorunoyo. Everything here comes from the organiser, the city, Osanbashi’s operator, the railway, the national observatory and the weather agency, plus one local site and two timetable sites where marked, as of 28 November 2026.

Nine shows, five minutes each

The show starts at 17:00, 17:30, 18:00, 18:30, 19:00, 19:30, 20:00, 20:30 and 21:00. That is 45 minutes of show in an evening of 4 hours and 5 minutes, and 243 shows over the 27 nights. Miss one and the next starts 25 minutes after it ends.

The organiser says the show covers more than 3 km of waterfront, from Minato Mirai to Yamashita Park. Its partner list has 50 entries, a record. They include Yokohama Landmark Tower, the Cosmo Clock 21 Ferris wheel, Yokohama Marine Tower (横浜マリンタワー), City Hall (横浜市庁舎) and the Yokohama Customs building. One of the 50 moves: the restaurant cruise ship Marine Rouge.

Older pages say 47. That was the number in the organiser’s first press release, on 4 September. The list on its site now has 50.

This is the 7th Yorunoyo, by the city’s count. The September press release puts last year’s attendance at 1 million visitors in total.

Where to stand

Four venues have installations from 17:00 to 21:05 every night, and all four are free.

The walking times in the first two rows are the organiser’s. The times for Nippon Maru Memorial Park come from the city’s visitor guide.

Osanbashi is a passenger-ship terminal about 430 m long and about 70 m wide. Its rooftop is a public deck, open 24 hours, and 15 m above the ground at its highest point. The organiser calls it the main viewpoint for the show.

Qujiland is the organiser’s own English spelling. Kujira is Japanese for whale.

In Yamashita Park, the organiser lights Marine Tower and Hikawa Maru (氷川丸), the ship moored at the park, together with Y Cube during each show. Hikawa Maru sounds its horn at the 17:00 show and at no other.

Nippon Maru gets projection mapping for the first time at Yorunoyo. The organiser says it can be watched from any side, 360 degrees. Its page does not say how long one run lasts.

Until 25 December there is a fifth venue. Christmas Gate, in the square called Shinko Chuo Hiroba (新港中央広場), is free and open from 11:00 to 21:05, with last food orders at 20:30. From 4 December its tree is lit in time with every show. The tree in the park of the Red Brick Warehouse (赤レンガ倉庫) is linked to three of them: 17:00, 19:00 and 21:00.

There is one indoor room. The Citizens’ Lounge (市民ラウンジ) on the 3rd floor of City Hall normally closes at 20:00. During Yorunoyo it stays open until 21:15, from 7:00 on weekdays and from 10:00 on weekends and holidays.

The organiser says the night view of Minato Mirai, lit by the show, can be seen from the Citizens’ Lounge as if in a picture frame. A note under the page’s photo says the construction of a nearby high-rise has changed part of that view.

Which show to pick

Take 17:00 for the horn. With small children it is also the earliest way home.

Sunset in Yokohama is at 16:28 from 4 to 7 December, the earliest of the month, and at 16:38 on 30 December, by the National Astronomical Observatory’s table. So the 17:00 show starts 32 minutes after sunset on opening night and 22 minutes after it on the last night.

The 17:30 show starts 52 to 62 minutes after sunset. The table has no twilight times, so pick by how much light you want left in the sky.

On three nights there is extra lighting between the shows. On Saturday 5 and Sunday 6 December it is red, themed on Coca-Cola, at 15 and 45 minutes past each hour for 5 minutes each time. On Wednesday 9 December the same slots turn blue, 100 days before GREEN×EXPO 2027 opens. The organiser’s page does not say which buildings take part.

Christmas Gate closes on 25 December. So does the Christmas market at the Red Brick Warehouse, which charges ¥500 or ¥1,000 to enter. An earlier piece on this site lists its price by date.

Yorunoyo then runs five more nights, 26 to 30 December, with the same nine shows. None of the sources read gives crowd figures by date.

An evening by the clock

Two shows cover the two venues the city’s press release names, Yamashita Park and Osanbashi.

  1. 16:45: arrive at Motomachi-Chukagai. Exit 4 is 3 minutes from Yamashita Park.
  2. 17:00: watch from the park for the horn, Y Cube and Marine Tower.
  3. 17:05: walk to Osanbashi. The local site yokohama-minatomirai.com allows about 15 minutes between the two with crowds.
  4. 17:30: watch from the rooftop. Stay on for Qujiland, or for the 18:00 show.
  5. Leave from Nihon-odori, 7 minutes from Osanbashi.

To add Nippon Maru, ride one stop from Nihon-odori to Bashamichi and walk 5 minutes. The other way is on foot through Zou-no-hana Park and along Kishamichi, under the searchlights. The organiser says you can walk between Minato Mirai and Yamashita Park while watching them. It gives no walking time, and none was measured.

What it costs

Entry is ¥0 at every venue on the organiser’s pages.

The Minatomirai Line from Yokohama Station costs ¥230 to Motomachi-Chukagai and ¥230 back from Nihon-odori. That is ¥460, or ¥448 by IC card. The hop from Nihon-odori to Bashamichi is ¥200, and Bashamichi to Yokohama is ¥200, so the three-venue evening is ¥630. The two railway fare pages checked show no child fares.

The last show ends at 21:05. On the Saturday timetable for 28 November, as two timetable sites print it, 11 trains leave Nihon-odori toward Yokohama between 21:05 and 21:59. The first is at 21:10, and the ride takes 6 or 7 minutes. Weekday times and the last train were not checked.

The organiser also promotes a Yorunoyo-design Minato Burari Ticket (みなとぶらりチケット) at ¥700 for adults and ¥350 for children. Its page says the ticket covers city buses, the municipal subway and Kanachu buses. The Minatomirai Line is not on that list.

By car, Osanbashi has 400 spaces, open 24 hours, at ¥500 for the first hour and ¥250 for each 30 minutes after it. From 1 December 2026 the operator removes the daily cap on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays. The weekday cap stays at ¥1,500 per 24 hours.

So 4 hours on a Saturday is ¥2,000 and 5 hours is ¥2,500. The fee table on the same page, dated 1 April 2022, still prints the old weekend cap of ¥2,000. The car park does not take motorcycles or bicycles.

Yamashita Park has 222 paid spaces. The city’s page gives no fee, and other car parks were not checked.

What is not good

The show is 5 minutes long. The other 25 minutes of each half hour are waiting or walking.

Osanbashi is the recommended venue, and a crowded one. The local site calls it “almost always crowded” and advises arriving 5 to 10 minutes before a show. The city’s page on Osanbashi says entry limits may be set on the rooftop when events are held. The organiser’s pages do not mention entry limits.

It is cold and exposed. The weather agency’s December normals for Yokohama are a mean daily low of 5.2°C, a high of 12.5°C and a mean wind of 3.4 m/s, most often from the north. The rooftop is an open wooden deck, and the operator warns that a fall on it can leave splinters.

Indoors is limited. Osanbashi’s 2nd-floor lobby is open from 9:00 to 21:30, which is 25 minutes after the last show ends.

The organiser’s top page and its seven programme pages say nothing about rain, wind, toilets or tripods. They are also silent on where the music can be heard. The local site says it is audible at Osanbashi and in Yamashita Park.

English pages disagree on one date. A page on haveagood-holiday.com, dated 6 November, ends the show on 25 December in one section. The organiser’s page says 30 December. Live Japan and Time Out, both dated 17 November, give the 30-minute interval and leave out the horn.

The PDF attached to the city’s press release could not be opened, so it was not read.

Who should go, and when

With children: 17:00 in Yamashita Park, 17:30 on the rooftop, and back at Nihon-odori Station by about 17:45.

For photographs: 17:00 is 22 to 32 minutes after sunset, and 18:00 is 82 to 92.

For the extra colour: 5, 6 or 9 December.

Without the Christmas market next door: 26 to 30 December.

By car: a weekday, when the cap is ¥1,500. On a weekend, take the train.

In rain: the organiser’s pages do not say what happens. The Citizens’ Lounge is indoors and open until 21:15.

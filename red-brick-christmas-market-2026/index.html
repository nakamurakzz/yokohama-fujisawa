Yokohama's Red Brick Christmas market: ¥500 on 27 days, ¥1,000 on 9

For anyone deciding whether to go and on which date: the days that cost double, what is free without a ticket, what one evening adds up to, and why the car is a bad idea.

The Christmas market at Yokohama Red Brick Warehouse (横浜赤レンガ倉庫) runs until 25 December 2026 and charges for entry: ¥500 on 27 of its 36 days and ¥1,000 on the other 9. This weekend, 28 and 29 November, is the last weekend at ¥500. Every Saturday and Sunday from 5 December costs double.

Two areas beside the market are free, and one of them is on the way in from Sakuragicho Station (桜木町).

I have been to this market. It was too crowded, and I don’t go any more. I also remember wandering around because I couldn’t park the car.

So this piece is for deciding whether you need to go at all, and on which date. Everything else in it comes from the organiser’s pages and three Japanese reports from the opening days, as of 26 November 2026.

The price depends on the date

The organiser sorts the dates into normal days (通常日) and special days (特定日). A normal ticket is ¥500 on a normal day and ¥1,000 on a special day, and the ticket list has no child or student price.

The 9 special days are 5, 6, 12, 13, 18, 19, 20, 24 and 25 December. That is every Saturday and Sunday left after this weekend, plus Friday 18, Thursday 24 and Friday 25 December. Every other day until 25 December is a normal day, which from today leaves 21 days at ¥500.

The market opens at 11:00. It closes at 21:00 until 4 December and at 22:00 from 5 December, with last orders 30 minutes before closing.

Children of elementary-school age and younger enter free all season, with a guardian. Until 4 December, junior-high students are free too. The market’s page does not spell out what a junior-high student pays from 5 December. It lists one price per kind of day.

So 2 adults with 2 elementary-school children pay ¥1,000 on a normal day and ¥2,000 on a special day.

The free day for Yokohama residents was 25 November. It has passed.

At the venue, the ticket is cash only. Same-day normal tickets are sold for cash at the main entrance (メインエントランス), and the ticket site Asoview (アソビュー！) sells them for other kinds of payment. An Asoview ticket does not shorten the wait: by the market’s page, those buyers queue at the normal gate with everyone else.

The priority ticket (優先入場チケット) has its own gate. It costs ¥2,000 on a normal day and ¥2,500 on a special day, and it is sold for a fixed date, in limited numbers, in advance on Asoview only. On either kind of day that is ¥1,500 more than the normal ticket.

A priority ticket that comes with a Noritake mug is ¥4,500 or ¥5,000. That mug is not sold separately.

What is free without a ticket

The market has about 60 food and goods stalls, and entry is charged for the main venue. The organiser lists two more areas, both free to enter.

Christmas Gate is in Shinko Chuo Hiroba (新港中央広場), a square the organiser describes as on the way from the Sakuragicho direction to the warehouse. It has a live tree about 8 m tall from Tokachi in Hokkaido, 18 shops and a children’s corner with a wood-chip sandbox. Payment in this area is cashless only.

Christmas Gate’s page prints two opening times. One section says 16:00 on weekdays until 18 December and 11:00 on weekends and holidays, while the summary on the same page says 11:00 every day.

Iko-yo, a family-outing site that covered opening day, also gives 16:00 on weekdays until 18 December. Plan for 16:00. Christmas Gate closes at 21:05.

Christmas Village is in Aka-Renga Park (赤レンガパーク), next to the main venue. It is open 11:00 to 21:00 until 4 December and 11:00 to 22:00 from 5 December. A Pokémon illumination has been running there since 25 November. One part of it, a tree decorated with the Pokémon you choose, needs a numbered ticket handed out on site from 11:00 each day, one per person.

The main tree is a live Sakhalin spruce (アカエゾマツ) about 11 m tall, with about 20,000 LEDs. It is lit from 16:00 to 23:00, so it stays on for an hour or two after the market shuts. From 16:00 it runs a light show of about 5 minutes every 15 minutes. From 5 December there are no shows from 22:00.

From 4 December, three shows a day, at 17:00, 19:00 and 21:00, are linked to Yorunoyo (ヨルノヨ), the illumination event on Yokohama’s central waterfront. The city’s press release dates Yorunoyo from 4 to 30 December, 17:00 to 21:05.

The organiser’s pages read for this piece do not say whether the main tree can be seen without a ticket, and nobody went to look. Its venue map, a separate booklet, was not read. A local site, Minato to Mirai, describes the way in from the priority gate as heading toward the tree in the centre of the venue.

The two brick buildings are free to walk into. Warehouse 1 (1号館) is open 10:00 to 19:00 and Warehouse 2 (2号館) from 11:00 to 20:00, so both close before the market does.

What an evening costs

Take the Minatomirai Line from Yokohama Station to Bashamichi (馬車道). The fare is ¥200, or ¥193 by IC card, and the warehouse’s access page gives about 6 minutes on foot. Nihon-odori (日本大通り), the next station, is also about 6 minutes on foot and costs ¥230. Use Bashamichi.

The same page gives about 12 minutes on foot from Minatomirai Station (みなとみらい) and about 15 minutes from Kannai (関内) or from Sakuragicho by the Kishamichi promenade (汽車道).

The organiser’s web pages and press release name dishes without prices, and its food booklet was not read for this piece. The prices below come from two Japanese reports, CAKE.TOKYO’s, updated on 24 November, and Iko-yo’s, dated 25 November.

For one adult on a normal day, the train both ways is ¥400, entry ¥500, a mulled wine ¥880 and a Krapfen ¥490. That is ¥2,270. On a special day it is ¥2,770.

I don’t drink alcohol. Swap the wine for a ¥1,000 milk tea and the normal-day total is ¥2,390.

Two adults who each have a mulled wine and share the sausage plate spend ¥5,360 on a normal day, train included.

The market’s own mug costs ¥900, in white or red, with a limit of 2 per person. The food stalls sell it only as a set with a drink. The information desk by the normal gate sells it too.

Inside, the stalls take cash and cashless payment. You cannot top up electronic money on site, and the ATM is on the ground floor of Warehouse 2. Bring cash for the ticket and a card or phone for Christmas Gate.

Food and drink bought elsewhere cannot be brought in. The tables and chairs are outdoors, and the market’s toilets are on the road side of Warehouse 1.

By car: 179 spaces

The warehouse has two car parks, with 83 and 96 spaces. The first hour costs ¥500 and each 30 minutes after that ¥250, so 3 hours is ¥1,500. The access page lists no daily maximum.

For scale, the organiser puts the 2025 market at about 1.21 million visitors, a figure it calculates from visitor numbers at Warehouses 1 and 2.

Spending ¥3,000 in one shop inside the buildings earns 1 hour of free parking. Purchases at the market do not count. The access page excludes outdoor events, and the market’s FAQ excludes shopping in the venue.

The organiser expects congestion at the car parks and on the roads around them on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, its access page says, and asks visitors to come by public transport. The same page adds that the car parks may be unavailable when full.

For two wheels, the warehouse FAQ puts the bicycle and motorcycle parking beside car park 1, by Warehouse 2. It gives no count and no fee.

Other car parks nearby were not checked for this piece.

What is not good

The queue comes first. The organiser says it may restrict entry when the venue is crowded, and that you may wait to get in however you bought your ticket.

Minato to Mirai’s writer went on opening day, Friday 20 November, and reported about 30 minutes to get in through the normal gate at around 17:30. That was a ¥500 day, on which the market had opened at 17:00. At about 19:00 the same writer found almost nobody at the priority gate, which is in a different place from the normal gate and nearer the stations.

There are no waiting times for the special days here. The organiser charges double on them.

The same writer warns about the sea wind at night, and had trouble finding a place to sit and eat on a weekday night last year. Iko-yo’s advice for the cold is to go into the buildings, which close at 19:00 and 20:00, one to three hours before the market does.

The organiser caps several dishes at 100 or 200 portions a day and one stall’s German white mulled wine at 30 cups a day, its press release says.

The market’s page is silent on re-entry. Assume that leaving means paying again.

English pages are behind. Time Out’s page, dated 27 November 2025, gives ¥500, a 12 m tree and 22:00 closing from 6 December. Tokyo Cheapo’s listing says ¥500 at the door. TripZilla’s 2026 guide, dated 29 September, gives a range of ¥500 to ¥1,000 and says the prices for each date had not been released.

Not checked for this piece: the skating rink the market’s page links to, the organiser’s food booklet and venue map, last trains, and how long the wait is on special days.

Who should go, and when

With children, or on a budget: go by 4 December. This weekend is ¥500, junior-high students are free, and the market shuts at 21:00.

For the lights only: walk in from Sakuragicho by way of Christmas Gate, then go on to Christmas Village. Neither charges entry. From 4 December the main tree’s shows at 17:00, 19:00 and 21:00 are linked to Yorunoyo.

On a special day: buy the ¥2,500 priority ticket in advance, or plan to stand in line at the normal gate.

By car: there are 179 spaces. Take the train.

If an acquaintance visiting from abroad asked me for one place here, I would take them to a small izakaya, a pub that serves food, in Noge (野毛) or around Yokohama Station or Bandobashi (阪東橋). I assume they will go to the Red Brick Warehouse anyway.

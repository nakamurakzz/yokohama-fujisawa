Enoshima's winter lights start on 1 December: dates, hours and prices

Shonan no Hoseki opens nine days later than last season. What it costs after 17:00, which nights run to 21:00, and the train from Fujisawa Station.

Enoshima’s winter illumination, Shonan no Hoseki (湘南の宝石), starts on Tuesday 1 December 2026 and runs to Sunday 28 February 2027. Last season it opened on 22 November. If you planned a late-November evening around an older guide, the lights will be off.

The short version: the lights come on at 17:00. The main display is inside a garden at the top of the island, which costs ¥500 from 17:00. Going up the tower in the middle of it is listed as a further ¥800. From Fujisawa Station the train takes 7 minutes and costs ¥170, and then you walk for about 20 minutes.

This piece is desk research. Nobody visited for it. The dates, hours and prices come from three Japanese event listings that carry the organiser’s announcement, and they agree with each other.

The dates, and why older pages are wrong

This is the 27th edition. It was recognised in 2025 as one of Japan’s three great illuminations, which is why it turns up in most winter lists for the Tokyo area.

English lists can be a season behind. One English listing checked for this piece still gives the start as late November. Last season that was right: 22 November 2025 to 28 February 2026. This season the first night is 1 December, nine days later.

The lights are in three places: Enoshima Samuel Cocking Garden (江の島サムエル・コッキング苑) at the top of the island, other spots on the island, and the Katase coast on the mainland side. The listings name two fees, both inside the garden. They do not say whether the island and coast displays cost anything, so that is not stated here either.

Lighting hours change four times

On an ordinary weekday the lights run from 17:00 to 20:00. On Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays they run to 21:00.

From Saturday 19 December to Wednesday 30 December they run to 21:00 every night, weekdays included.

From Thursday 31 December to Sunday 3 January they stop at 20:00 every night, even on the weekend.

From Saturday 16 January the start moves to 17:30, for weekdays and weekends alike.

The listings for this season give no last-entry time. Last season it was 30 minutes before the lights went off: 19:30 on weekdays and 20:30 on weekends. Plan as if that still applies.

What it costs

There are two fees.

Entry to the garden is free until 17:00. From 17:00 it is ¥500 for adults and ¥250 for children.

The Sea Candle is the lighthouse tower inside the garden. Its fee is listed as ¥800 for adults and ¥400 for children. Its viewing floor is 41 metres above the ground and 101 metres above the sea.

The listings show these as two fees for two things. If both are charged in full, an adult who enters the garden and goes up the tower pays ¥1,300. No combined ticket price was confirmed for this piece.

Whether someone who walks into the garden at 16:50 is asked to pay at 17:00 was not confirmed either. Do not build your evening on that.

The train from Fujisawa Station is cheap. The Odakyu Enoshima Line to Katase-Enoshima (片瀬江ノ島) is ¥170 each way, ¥167 with an IC card. So the evening from Fujisawa Station comes to:

Getting there from Fujisawa Station and from Yokohama

Fujisawa Station (藤沢) is the place to change. From Yokohama Station it is 20 minutes on the JR Tokaido Line. From Shonandai it is 7 minutes by Odakyu express and ¥200.

At Fujisawa you choose between two trains.

The Odakyu Enoshima Line takes 7 minutes to Katase-Enoshima and costs ¥170. Katase-Enoshima is the closest station to the island: about 20 minutes on foot to the garden.

The Enoden takes about 10 minutes to Enoshima Station (江ノ島) and costs ¥220. From there the walk to the garden is about 25 minutes. The two stations are 11 minutes apart on foot.

The Shonan Monorail’s Shonan-Enoshima Station is a third way in, about 26 minutes on foot from the garden.

For this trip the Odakyu line is ¥50 cheaper each way and leaves you five minutes closer. Take the Enoden if the Enoden itself is part of what you came for.

Train times and fares above are from route searches. The Fujisawa to Katase-Enoshima fares come from searches dated July to September 2026, and the others from 19 November 2026.

What is not good

It is a 20-minute walk before you see the main display, and it ends in a climb. The tower’s viewing floor is 101 metres above the sea and 41 metres above the ground, which puts the garden about 60 metres up.

On a weekday you have three hours, 17:00 to 20:00. From Shinjuku, the routes checked took between 62 and 112 minutes to Katase-Enoshima. Leave Tokyo after work and a large part of the three hours goes on trains.

The organiser extends the hours every night from 19 to 30 December. That is a fair sign of when the most people are expected. If you can choose, the first two and a half weeks of December and the weeks after mid-January are the other options, and from 16 January you lose the first half hour.

It is an island, in winter, after dark. Nothing in the listings says what happens in bad weather.

The organiser’s own page could not be opened when this piece was checked. Before you travel, confirm the hours and fees on the official Shonan no Hoseki page (Japanese).

If you only remember three things

The first night is 1 December.

Bring ¥500 for the garden, and ¥800 more if you want the tower.

From Fujisawa Station, take the Odakyu line to Katase-Enoshima.

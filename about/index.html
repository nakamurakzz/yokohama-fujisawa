About this site

Who writes it, how it is made, and what happens when it is wrong.

Who writes this

I’m Kaz. I live in Yokohama with my family, in a part of the city I call its countryside.

I work as a software engineer at a sports-technology startup. I became an engineer at 30, after years as a project manager. I have two daughters. At 35 I rode a motorbike for the first time, and now I ride a 125cc Honda Monkey on back roads. I carry a small camera most days.

English is my second language.

How each piece is made

You should know what you are reading, so here it is plainly.

My notes start in Japanese. A writing system I set up, built on an AI model (Claude, made by Anthropic), turns them into English and researches the topic. It checks every price, time, date and opening hour against an official source on the day of publication. A second, separate AI pass then checks the facts again from scratch and scores the piece. If the piece fails, it is not published that day.

Pieces that pass both checks are published automatically.

Two rules hold this together.

  1. No invented experience. A sentence that says “I” comes from my own notes. When a piece has none of my notes behind it, the box at the bottom says it is desk research.
  2. No unsourced numbers. Every piece lists what it was checked against, and when.

What “Actually” means

Each piece opens with a box of numbers: what the thing costs, how long it takes, when to go, and who should skip it. If you read nothing else, read the box.

Each piece also has a section on what is not good. Crowds, stairs, prices, weather. I would rather you arrive knowing.

When something is wrong

Prices and timetables change, and a checking system can still miss things. If you find a mistake, report it here. The piece gets fixed, and the fix is dated at the bottom of the piece.

Money
